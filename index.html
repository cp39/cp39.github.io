Economics · Peking University

Jianduo Li 李健铎

I am a Ph.D. student in Economics at the National School of Development (China Center for Economic Research), Peking University.

My research is in applied microeconomics. I study how people influence one another in networks, classrooms, and teams—and how those interactions shape the effects of policy.

My current work focuses on heterogeneous peer effects, the formation and rewiring of social networks, and the consequences of generative AI for team production. I am advised by Professor Junjian Yi.

Research

Current interests

  • Peer effects & networks

    Heterogeneous social interactions, network endogeneity, and the design of peer-group and network policies.

  • Team production & AI

    How technology and incentives change team formation, effort allocation, helping behavior, and collaborative output.

  • Applied microeconomics

    Empirical work in the economics of education and technology, combining credible variation with transparent behavioral models.
