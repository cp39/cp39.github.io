Research

Questions about peers, teams, and technology.

I combine randomized experiments and natural variation with structural estimation to study how social interactions shape behavior and policy outcomes.

Areas

Research interests

  • Peer effects across domains

    I study how peers' characteristics, choices, and outcomes shape individual behavior in classrooms, workplaces, and other social environments. My work asks when standard peer means are inadequate and how nonlinear influence changes policy design.

  • Teamwork and team production

    I study how teams form, how members divide time between their own tasks and helping others, and how incentives and generative AI change collaboration.

  • Applied microeconomics

    More broadly, I work on the economics of education and technology, especially questions where credible exogenous variation can be paired with a transparent behavioral model.

In progress

Working papers

  1. 01

    Heterogeneous Peer Effects in Social Networks: Structural Estimation and Policy Simulations

    with Junjian Yi · Working paper

    This paper asks whether the equal-weight peer mean adequately describes a student's peer environment. Using friend nominations, repeated Chinese and mathematics scores, and a randomized seating intervention, we distinguish a direct payoff spillover from adjustment toward a peer-based reference point and allow that reference point to depend nonlinearly on peers' effort. Counterfactual simulations compare targeted link addition, degree-preserving rewiring, random link addition, and a persistent productivity intervention for bottom-quintile students.

    Peer effects · Social networks · Heterogeneous peer effects · Structural estimation

  2. 02

    Joint Tasks, Information Disclosure, and Friendship Rewiring: A Theory of Social Integration and Academic Spillovers

    with Zhi Cao and Junjian Yi · Working paper

    This paper develops a theory in which joint tasks reshape friendship networks by revealing previously unknown common interests. Friendships require bilateral consent and social attention is scarce, so a newly attractive tie can displace an existing one. The model separates direct contact from information disclosure and yields predictions for link formation, persistence, dissolution, reciprocity, and academic spillovers.

    Friendship formation · Information disclosure · Network rewiring · Segregation

  3. 03

    How Generative AI Changes Team Production: Motivation, Experimental Design, and Testable Predictions

    with Dengkun Chen and Junjian Yi · Working paper (experimental design)

    We propose an experiment that separates AI's direct productivity effect from changes in team formation, effort allocation, and helping behavior. The design crosses AI access with team-formation rules and payment rules, and measures both fluid intelligence and social skill. The resulting predictions concern solo productivity, helping under team pay, AI-by-payment interactions, and mutual team formation.

    Generative AI · Team production · Incentives · Helping behavior · Experimental design
